\en-abstract{
This research presents the design, fabrication, calibration, and evaluation of a soft, multi-modal, bio-inspired tactile sensor with the aim of enriching the interactive capabilities of robots. Drawing inspiration from the multi-modal nature of human touch, which perceives static and dynamic information with different receptors, this study addresses a novel approach to overcome the limitations of single-modality sensors. The research began with the design and fabrication of a single-axis prototype based on a barometric pressure sensor, which not only proved the efficacy of this mechanism for accurate normal force measurement but was also evaluated for its high linearity and low hysteresis properties. As this prototype lacked the capability for multi-modal sensing and tangential force measurement, an integrated multi-modal module was developed. This module utilizes a barometric sensor, two Hall effect sensors with an embedded magnet to resolve tangential forces, and a MEMS microphone acting as a piezoelectric sensor to capture high-frequency vibrations from slip and surface texture. To manage and synchronize the large volume of heterogeneous data, a dedicated data acquisition board was designed with innovative features such as composite USB communication protocol and advanced utilization of Direct Memory Access (DMA). Furthermore, due to the sensor's inherently nonlinear and complex nature, the calibration process was performed using deep learning models. A fully connected neural network was trained to model the static behavior and accurately extract force vectors, while a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural network was trained to characterize the sensor's dynamic and time-dependent properties. The accuracy of the presented system was found to be \lr{99.75\%} for normal force and \lr{98.1\%} for shear force. Finally, the overall system's efficacy was demonstrated in a practical implementation. The sensor was mounted on a robotic gripper, and within a control loop, successfully showcased its ability to perform precise grasp force control while simultaneously detecting dynamic phenomena through vibrational data. In conclusion, these achievements provide a comprehensive hardware and software framework for advancing robotic tactile perception while enabling intelligent and safe interactions with objects.
}
